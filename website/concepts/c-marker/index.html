How it works

Three beats.

  1. 1Draw

    Your pointer becomes a pen, on top of whatever app is open.

  2. 2Use apps

    Click and type in the app underneath again. Your drawings stay where you left them.

  3. this in the way goes here

    3Fix it

    Every mark stays editable. Move it, bend it, change its color, lock it, undo it.

Press the keys. This page starts and stops drawing.

  • ⌃Ctrl2 Show or hide drawings
  • ⌃Ctrl1 Zoom
  • ⌃Ctrl3 Break timer
  • ⌃CtrlW Whiteboard
  • ⌃CtrlB Blackboard
  • ⌘CtrlL Tool library
  • Single keys pick tools while you draw: 5 Arrow, 7 Pen, N Numbered counter. You can change every key in Settings.

Not in the rules

Things you don't expect.

No. 1

Your wobbly loop comes out neat.

Turn on Smart Draw and sketch with the pen. A wobbly loop or a shaky arrow snaps into a clean one.

No. 2

Share one corner of a wide screen.

Sharing a single window can leave your drawings out, and nobody wants your whole ultrawide. Share region puts one part of a display in its own window. Your meeting app shares that window.

No. 3

Back in ten.

The break timer fills the screen with a countdown, from 1 to 180 minutes, with a title and a chime. Out of the box it's a 10-minute coffee break.

Coffee break10:00

No. 4

A toolbar that gets out of the way.

Make it horizontal or vertical, add or remove buttons, and when you leave it alone it shrinks to one button.

No. 5

Shapes you draw once and reuse.

Save any drawing or text to My shapes and drop it in again later. You can paste images too.

No. 6

A pointer people can follow.

Give the cursor a halo and choose its shape, color and glow. Or point with the laser: its trail fades on its own.

No. 7

An AI assistant can drive it.

An optional skill teaches an AI app that already has computer-use tools to use Annotator the way you do: pick a tool, draw, edit, check. It adds no permissions of its own. The AI provider may receive screenshots under its own privacy settings.

Straight answers

The fine print, in big print.

Will people on my call see my drawings?

It depends.

Share your whole screen and they see them. We checked that by hand with Teams on a Mac. Share one app or window and the drawings may be left out, so ask someone on the call what they see. Share region is there for that case.

Does it record or upload my screen?

No.

Annotator is not a screen recorder and saves no video. Your drawings, screen pixels and typed text stay on your computer. It does contact a release server to check for updates.

Is blur safe for secrets?

No.

Blur, Pixelate and Distort cover a region live, but details can stay readable. The cover doesn't follow a window that moves or a page that scrolls, and it's gone when you hide or quit Annotator. Remove private details before you share.

Does it need permissions?

For drawing, none.

Screen zoom, the live effects and Share region need screen access: Screen Recording on a Mac, the screenshots and screen recording setting on Windows.

Is it the same on Mac and Windows?

Yes.

It's one product with two native apps. The Windows app matches the Mac app, and where they differ, the difference is written down.

Does it work on more than one display?

Yes.

Each display has its own drawings.

What if I draw the wrong thing?

Undo it.

Undo and redo cover everything, including Clear and the Eraser. Lock a mark and neither of those can remove it.

Can I keep what I drew?

Yes.

Save a board and open it later with every mark still editable, or export it as a PNG.

Is it finished?

Not yet.

The latest release is 0.5.0, and it hasn't reached 1.0. Expect rough edges, and tell us when you find one.

And the big one: is it really free?

Free.

  • All tools are free.
  • No account.
  • No paid tiers.
  • Coffee is optional.

Usage counts are off unless you turn them on, and they never include drawings, screenshots or text.

Or with Homebrew

brew install --cask dorlugasigal/annotator/annotator
  1. 1Open the disk image and drag Annotator to Applications.
  2. 2Open it. It lives in the menu bar and updates itself.
  3. 3Press ⌃⇧2 and draw. The built-in tutorial shows you the rest.
