Fig. 3 — six moments

When would I use it?

Whenever someone has to find the thing you’re talking about. Here are six questions you can answer with a drawing.

  1. Walking the team through numbers

    Which number dropped?

    Circle it, then point an arrow at the day it happened. Nobody has to hunt across the dashboard.

    • Ellipse
    • Arrow

  2. Reviewing code

    Which line of code?

    Box the line and write your question next to it. If something private is on screen, cover it first.

    • Rectangle
    • Text
    • Arrow
    • Blur

  3. Teaching a class or a workshop

    What are the steps?

    Number them as you go and highlight the part people skip. A Whiteboard gives you a clean page when the slides are in the way.

    • Numbered counter
    • Highlighter
    • Whiteboard

  4. Recording a tutorial

    Where should I look?

    Dim everything except the part that matters, or zoom in around your pointer. A halo or a laser trail shows where you are between steps.

    • Spotlight
    • Screen zoom
    • Cursor halo
    • Laser pointer

  5. Explaining a system

    How do the parts connect?

    Sketch it on the spot. Draw boxes and arrows, or drop in ready-made icons for a database, a server or an API gateway.

    • Rectangle
    • Arrow
    • Database
    • Server
    • API gateway
    • Blackboard

  6. Helping someone on a support call

    Which button do I press?

    Show it on your screen. Ring the exact button and point at it, so “the one on the right” is never the wrong one.

    • Ellipse
    • Arrow
    • Text

Fig. 8 — the fine print

What’s the catch?

There isn’t one, but there are limits. Here are the straight answers.

Will people on my call see my drawings?
If you share your whole screen. Share one app or window and the drawings may be left out, so ask someone on the call what they see. Whole-screen sharing was checked by hand with Teams on a Mac.
Does it record or upload my screen?
No. Annotator is not a screen recorder and saves no video. Drawings, screen pixels and typed text stay on your computer. Update checks contact a release server, and usage counts stay off unless you turn them on. Read the privacy page
Is blur safe for secrets?
No. Blur, Pixelate and Distort cover an area live, but details can stay readable. The cover doesn’t follow a window that moves or scrolls, and it goes away when you hide or quit Annotator. Take real secrets off the screen first.
Is it really free?
Yes. No account. No paid tiers. Coffee is optional and unlocks nothing.
Is it the same on Mac and Windows?
Yes. It is one product with two native apps. The Windows app matches the Mac app, and where they differ, it is written down.
Is it finished?
Not yet. The latest release is 0.5.0, so it has not reached 1.0. Expect rough edges, and please report the ones you find.
Does it work on more than one display?
Yes. Each display has its own drawings and its own background.
Does it need special permissions?
Not for drawing. Screen zoom, live effects and Share region ask for screen access: Screen Recording on a Mac, the screenshots and screen recording setting on Windows.
Can I keep what I drew?
Yes. Save a board and open it later, still editable, or export it as a PNG.
