In the wings

Things you don't expect from a drawing app.

  1. Draw badly on purpose.

    Turn on Smart Draw and sketch a wobbly loop, a rough box or a shaky arrow with the Pen. It snaps into a clean one. You look like you practised.

  2. Share one corner of a very wide screen.

    Share region puts part of one display in its own window, and your meeting app shares that window. It is also the fix when sharing a single app leaves your drawings out.

  3. A toolbar that gets out of the way.

    Lay it flat or stand it up, add or remove buttons, pick one of two sizes. Leave it alone and it shrinks to a single button.

  4. A whiteboard that was your desktop a second ago.

    Swap the desktop for a whiteboard or a blackboard on that display, sketch the idea, then switch back. The app underneath never moved.

  5. Keep the drawings you make every week.

    Save any selection of drawings or text to My shapes. Next talk, drop it in with one click instead of drawing it again.

  6. A pointer nobody loses.

    Cursor halo wraps your pointer in a shape you choose: its color, opacity and glow, and an effect on every click so viewers see what you pressed.

  7. An AI assistant can hold the pen.

    An optional skill teaches an AI app that already has computer-use tools to work Annotator through its normal interface. It is documented for Copilot CLI and Claude Code, and it adds no permissions of its own. How AI control works

Before you install

Straight answers.

Will people on my call see my drawings?
If you share your whole screen, yes. If you share one app or one window, the drawings may be left out, so ask someone on the call what they see. Share region is made for that case. We checked whole-screen sharing by hand with Teams on a Mac; other meeting apps may behave differently.
Does it record or upload my screen?
No. Annotator is not a screen recorder and it does not save video. Your drawings, screen pixels and typed text stay on your computer. It does contact a release server to check for updates. Usage counts are off unless you turn them on, and they never include drawings, screenshots or text.
Is blur safe for secrets?
No. Blur, Pixelate and Distort cover a region live, but details can stay readable, the cover doesn't follow a window that moves or scrolls, and it is gone when you hide or quit Annotator. Close the secret before you share.
Is it really free?
Yes. All tools are free. No account. No paid tiers. It is open source under Apache 2.0. Coffee is optional and unlocks nothing.
Is it the same on Mac and Windows?
That is the rule we build by: one product, two native apps. The Windows app matches the Mac app, and the differences are written down in the repository.
Is it finished?
Not yet. The latest release is 0.5.0, and it has not reached 1.0. Expect rough edges. It updates itself on a Mac, and the Microsoft Store updates it on Windows.
Does it work on more than one display?
Yes. Each display has its own drawings, and each one can be the desktop, a whiteboard or a blackboard.
What permissions does it ask for?
Drawing needs none. Screen zoom, the live effects and Share region need screen access: Screen Recording on a Mac, the screenshots and screen recording setting on Windows.
Can I change the keys?
Yes, in Settings. While you draw, single keys pick tools (5 is Arrow, 7 is Pen, N is Numbered counter) and colors (R red, G green, B blue, Y yellow).
