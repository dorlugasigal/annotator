How to use Annotator.

What you can do now

Draw with a pen or highlighter, add shapes and arrows, and write text. You can move, resize, rotate, group or lock what you draw. Use Undo to reverse a change. Save your work as a board you can edit later, or as a PNG image.

Draw on any connected monitor without moving the toolbar. Click a canvas to make it the editing target. Click the Desk/White/Black toolbar button to cycle that monitor’s background. Control-W toggles Whiteboard; Control-B toggles Blackboard. Other monitors keep their own backgrounds.

Drag the toolbar until your pointer touches a screen edge. A ghost shows the centered dock position on that edge; release to place it there. Top and bottom use a horizontal layout; left and right use a vertical layout. Move the pointer even slightly away from the edge to place it freely. The small Draw/Apps control shows the input mode with both text and color.

Command-A selects drawings across displays. Properties, Delete and arrow-key movement apply to that selection together. Click a canvas to return to local selection for dragging and resizing.

The pen, highlighter and eraser cursors show the tool size before you draw. Hold Shift while highlighting for a straight line, snapped to horizontal, vertical or diagonal. Release Shift to continue freehand. Undo removes the whole stroke.

Use a spotlight to point out a detail, or zoom into part of the live desktop. Add numbered steps and measure distances while you explain. Live zoom and blur need Screen Recording permission. Option-scroll over other apps also needs Accessibility permission. Annotator does not have a separate image editor.

Change the tool order, shortcuts and toolbar position in Settings. The tool library has Draw, Shapes, Focus and Extras sections. Effects offers Blur, Pixelate and Distort. Zero Blur or Pixelate strength turns off the effect. Use Rectangle with a fill for a solid block. Older solid covers stay opaque when you open a saved board. Hold Shift while resizing to keep the same shape.

Covers stay where you place them. They do not follow windows or scrolling content. Move or resize a cover yourself when the content moves, and remove private details before a call. Public app downloads are not available yet. All current tools are free.

Save your own shapes

Tools opens in a resizable window. Drag an edge or corner to show more columns and rows, or scroll through the grid. Choosing a shape hides the window; Command-L opens it again.

Web is a globe. API gateway and Load balancer are separate symbols. Database uses stacked disks; Cylinder preserves the older simple shape. System symbols keep their proportions and use the same artwork in the library and on the desktop.

Labels that belong to shapes

With Select, double-click anywhere inside a shape, even when it has no fill. Or choose Text and click inside it. Type a label and press Escape or Command-Return to finish. The label is centered and follows the shape when you move, resize, rotate, copy or save it. Double-click again to edit the label.

Shift-click objects or drag a selection box to select several. Drag a selected object to move them together. Use the shared corner handles to resize them proportionally, or the top handle to rotate them. Locked objects stay fixed. A selection containing an unlocked cover cannot rotate. Undo reverses the whole move or transform.

Use Properties to change the selected shapes together. Fill color is also available in quick properties on a narrow or vertical toolbar. Locked shapes keep their settings. Changing fill leaves each shape’s stroke color and width unchanged.

Long labels can expand the shape around its center. While editing, text properties change the label rather than the shape’s outline. Boards containing these labels use a newer file format; older builds reject them instead of dropping the labels.

Select a drawing or group, then open Tool library > My shapes. Give it a name and click Save selection. Click a saved shape to add an editable copy in the center of your view. Move it where you need it. Deleting a saved shape does not delete copies you already placed.

You can save drawings and text. Images, covers and live effects cannot be saved as shapes.

Start automatically when you log in

Open Settings > General and turn on Launch at login. If approval is needed, click Open Login Items and allow Annotator in macOS settings. The app starts quietly at login; use its menu-bar icon or drawing shortcut when you need it. Turn the switch off to stop future login launches.

This setting belongs to your Mac account and is not included in exported profiles. Keep the app in a stable location, preferably Applications. Registration errors are shown rather than silently enabling a setting that does not work.

Choose how the app looks

In Settings, choose System, Light or Dark under Theme. This changes the app controls, not the colors of your drawings.

Anchored slide moves the toolbar and drawings outward beyond the docked screen edge when hiding, and reverses when showing. Quick, Reveal, Curtain, Fold and Rise return the drawing toward the toolbar before it leaves. Fade stays a simple fade. Choose Off for no animation. The app also follows macOS Reduce Motion.

Check what others can see

If you share your whole screen, others may see your drawings and covers. If you share just one app or window, they may not. Ask someone on the call to check before showing private information. Quitting Annotator, or an app crash, removes its covers.

Blur, pixelation and distortion can leave details readable. A filled rectangle is an annotation, not secure redaction. Remove private information from the material you plan to show, and check what the other person can see. Scrolling, moving a window or changing the page layout can move content outside an overlay.

The eraser can remove unlocked covers, and toolbar trash clears the canvas. Undo restores removed objects. The explicitly named Clear drawings (keep censors) command still preserves covers. Exported annotation-only PNGs do not include ambient desktop pixels; image exports deliberately include imported or pasted images.

Compatibility

We are building for macOS 14 and later on Apple Silicon. We still need to test older supported Macs, multiple screens, more keyboard languages and screen readers. We also need to check what people see in screen-sharing apps. Intel support and an App Store release are not promised.

Permissions

You can draw and edit imported images without screen access. Zoom and live blur need to see the screen. macOS calls this permission “Screen Recording,” even though Annotator does not record a video. To use Option-scroll over other apps, allow Annotator under Privacy & Security > Accessibility. The app does not use your microphone or camera.

Permission setup is in Settings > Permissions, not on the floating toolbar. If scrolling works only over Annotator while macOS already shows access enabled, check that the permission entry belongs to the exact app copy you are running. Re-add that copy and relaunch after approval; do not reset other apps’ permissions.
